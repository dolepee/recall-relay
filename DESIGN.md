## Overview

Help a merchant move from a sourced product notice to reviewed customer cases and a confirmed refund to the original payment. The connected sandbox surface uses fictional merchandise and real sandbox payments; the separate training surface uses invented payment IDs. Show that limitation beside the work, and never describe a prepared refund as money returned.

There are two surfaces in one deployment. The public landing document at `/` introduces the job and the claim workflow to a merchant who has not used the product. The workbench and the private customer claim keep `/desk`, `/setup` and `/claim`, and the training workspace keeps `/training`. Routing differs by environment on purpose: the Vite development server answers every path with the app document, the built local server answers `/` with the landing document and the four app paths with the app document, and Cloudflare Pages answers `/` with the landing document, `/desk`, `/setup` and `/claim` with the app document, and 404 for everything else. The training workspace is local-only and must stay unavailable on Pages. The landing page is a static document; every action on it either scrolls within the page or navigates to the workbench. It never simulates a refund, a form submission or a payment.


## Product shell overhaul — 2026-11-03

The homepage and practice surface are one product, not a marketing page plus a separate demo. Use one merchant shell: the white and cool-grey surfaces, navy ink, hairline and blue action palette above; one Helvetica-first display face; the same system text and monospace figures; one lockup; one navigation pattern; one button hierarchy (one filled primary, one outlined secondary, one text link). A mode chip changes the context, not the identity: `Practice · simulated`, `Sandbox · test funds`, `Customer view · practice`.

The public job is stated before the machinery: **Manage recall claims. Refund the affected item.** The three product stages are **Prepare claim → Review evidence → Approve refund**. Each stage has one sentence; technical or source detail is contextual, not a second tutorial. Correction is an exception inside Review evidence, never a fourth stage and never a mandatory-looking step. A clean practice case must offer approval; a second fictional case demonstrates the correction branch.

The homepage has one primary first-time action: **Try a practice claim**. **Open Sandbox workspace** is secondary. **Preview a sample claim** is a text link. The home page should show the claim record and settlement evidence rather than four equal entry buttons.

Practice uses the merchant shell and the same cards, stage bar, status language and controls. Its explicit simulated boundary is a persistent mode note beside the work. The customer role is a role handoff within that shell, not a separate visual product. Preserve the existing practice store, correction/revision states, review gate and simulated-refund boundary; do not move payment or approval semantics into the visual layer.

The credibility anchor is a plain settlement record at the decision point: affected amount, unaffected amount, human review, and provider-confirmed/simulated status. Never invent named approvers, logos, customer counts, certifications or production status. Do not describe a prepared refund as money returned.

Green `#0B6A3C` on `#E9F6EF` is reserved for actual confirmed success; amber is reserved for attention; red is reserved for failure. There are no cream or brown surfaces, including beneath the dark hero. The dark hero is deep navy with white text. The merchant shell uses the same Helvetica-first display face, system sans for text, and monospace for figures, references and timestamps as the landing page. Do not add animation, decorative charts, gradients, or a marketing hero inside the merchant task pages.

The private customer claim at `/claim`, practice and its customer role share the merchant shell. The customer shell drops merchant navigation and keeps the private-link context. This is still the same claim state machine, not a second visual product.

## Layout

For a returning merchant, start with a compact active-campaign identity and the claim queue. Keep the full notice and purchase connection available through progressive disclosure, rather than placing their complete text before the current work. First use has one primary route into notice preparation. Keep suggested product matches, affected cases, and the selected case within one workbench. On small screens, stack the notice, case list, and selected case in that order. The case detail names the exact order, evidence requirement, amount, and original payment before approval. AG Studio supports the case workspace; its configuration controls must not replace the merchant task.

There is no separate five-step tutorial, no second product, and no duplicated set of equal entry actions. The hero has one filled primary action (practice), one outlined secondary action (sandbox) and one text link (sample claim). The product preview is a labelled sandbox example, not a live control surface.

At 1440 the hero is a centred product statement above a wide operational claim desk preview. At 390 the statement and actions stack, the preview follows, and no element exceeds the viewport. The page has no nested scrolling. The stage strip is three columns at desktop and one column on small screens. Measured positions are review artifacts, not fixed values; re-measure after copy changes.

## Do's and Don'ts

The existing plan and journal modules distinguish reviewed scope, approval, reserved refunds, unresolved attempts, and provider-confirmed completion. Preserve those states in the interface. Purchase fixture records are invented; imported CPSC notices are public source material and cannot silently become approved policies for those purchases. The hosted sandbox keeps each browser's merchant workspace separate; local launch links remain a separate access method. The sponsor has explicitly allowed AG Studio's evaluation watermark during hackathon judging; retain it.

Keep customer claims separate from merchant controls. The customer page starts with the item and required next action, collects evidence inline, and retains a case status and receipt after submission. Merchant controls require the private local merchant session. Mark sandbox money and invented merchandise beside the transaction. The merchant reviews the exact evidence revision before the payment confirmation dialog. Never render a payment as completed before authenticated provider readback. The private customer claim at `/claim`, practice and its customer role, and the merchant `/desk` and `/setup` surfaces all use the shared merchant shell. The customer shell removes merchant navigation and keeps the private-link context beside the lockup.

Keep notice preparation and case resolution as distinct merchant steps. Show proposed claim fields beside the exact source excerpt that supports them. The merchant reviews the current source version before saving instructions; a prepared setup is not a published campaign or payment authorization. The Studio assistant may change the case view and explain recorded blockers. It has no tool to approve evidence or send a refund.

Keep the merchant's ordinary case list and review controls usable while the optional interactive workspace downloads, or if it fails to load. Mobile uses those ordinary controls without loading Studio. The desktop Studio workspace opens in its supported view mode, with case review given more room than the queue. Workspace editing and the assistant are explicitly opened secondary tools. It retains its grid and custom review widget once ready; both reuse the same review and payment controls and server state.

The landing page may be reviewed as a marketing surface, but it must not grow a second product: no simulated refund, no fake form submission, no invented demo fixture and no submission copy. Adding a claim, changing an amount or changing the workflow belongs in the workbench, where the recorded plan, journal and provider states already exist. Keep `/landing.html` unreachable directly, with the document served only at `/`, and keep the workbench routes serving the app document. Keep `/training` absent from the Pages route list, and describe the environments accurately in the README and in review notes rather than claiming that every environment routes identically.

Publishing a reviewed source creates a sandbox scenario for future test purchases. Freeze each purchase’s campaign and evidence requirements when the purchase is created. Preserve earlier pending purchases and claims when the active scenario changes. Show the exact source-required responses to both the customer and the merchant; missing responses block evidence review. A public notice used in a sandbox scenario does not imply manufacturer affiliation.

Evidence review must include a way to ask the customer for a correction. A merchant names the specific problem and whether a replacement photograph is required. Show the request prominently on the same private customer page, with the previous submission retained. Updating a requested photograph requires a new upload; a customer describes their response before resubmitting. This is a request for reviewable information, not an automated denial or an authenticity decision. Clear the prior evidence approval immediately, require a new review after the response, and prevent corrections once a payment is reserved. Both parties see the latest request and response; no email is sent by this feature.

A new merchant with no claims should see a clear first action to prepare a source-backed claim form. Explain the sequence in plain language before the optional fictional example. In the active desk, separate ordinary submitted evidence awaiting review from exceptional manual cases and from reviewed claims awaiting payment approval. The assistant’s wording and case views must use the same distinctions; finding a queue never approves its contents.

Customer page headings and lead content follow the recorded state. An open claim leads with the evidence action, a correction leads with the merchant request, a submitted claim names the merchant as the next actor, and a completed claim leads with its verified receipt. General submission instructions must not remain above a completed refund. Keep source instructions, previous responses and the scenario disclosure available as supporting context.

Source setup exposes the current stage: import, review fields, or publish. After instructions are saved, compress earlier drafting into an explicit edit affordance and bring publication forward. Keep unsupported-route reasons next to the blocked action, and expose saved-setup loading failures with a retry.

## Practice case

`/practice` is a local evaluation surface inside the shared product shell: one fictional $25.99 item inside a $59.99 practice purchase, with the unaffected $34.00 line excluded. On small screens the page opens as a compact current-stage bar, one money line and the active task card; the full three-stage strip and full settlement table appear at wider widths or at the review/approval decision. The practice dialog uses the same card, ink, hairline and blue action tokens through its portal; it must not fall back to the old white/teal popup. It reuses the claim state machine and these design tokens, but keeps its own store, session cookie and claim identifiers, so it cannot read or change a connected sandbox workspace. Reviewed requirements stay linked to the invented practice notice; the recorded assistant draft is labelled as recorded, not live. The three visible stages are Prepare claim, Review evidence and Approve refund; correction is a conditional branch inside review. Evidence review, a requested correction, a fresh review and a clearly simulated refund remain the whole journey. No payment provider, credentials, live model call or paid service is involved, and the receipt never claims that money moved. The practice and customer roles share the merchant shell; only the mode note and role handoff change.
